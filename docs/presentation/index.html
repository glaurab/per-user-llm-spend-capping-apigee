Per-user LLM
spend capping

One call, one cost — checked against the budget before the model runs, then settled to the exact amount once the answer has been priced.

What an answer costs is not known until the answer exists, so the decision to allow a call has to be taken before the expensive half of it has been priced. This gateway meters one request at two points against a single counter: on the way in it prices the input exactly and can still refuse, and on the way out it adds only the difference between that and the real cost — never more, and never a second refusal. It runs here as a simulation, in two steps: choose the situation, then walk the call through it and watch the counters move.

Step 1 of 2  ·  your choice

Pick the situation to simulate

A scenario fixes two things before the call is made: how much of this user's budget is already spent, and what they are about to ask for. Both are read straight into the counters in step 2, so choosing here re-seeds the meter and rebuilds the walkthrough from the beginning. The policies never change — only the outcome does.

Three things the meter is showing you

One policy, two attachments

The same three QU-* quota policies appear in the request flow and again in the response flow. One policy object attached at two steps shares one counter — that is what makes a split charge possible at all, and it is the first thing to verify against your own Apigee version.

Money, not tokens

Counters hold integers of one millionth of a currency unit, and prices are stored per million tokens, so cost is a bare multiplication. Under a token cap, a Pro call and a Flash-Lite call subtract the same amount from a budget they cost very differently.

Overshoot is a stated number

The gateway overwrites maxOutputTokens and clamps candidateCount to 1, so the furthest one call can push a caller past their cap is the output ceiling times the dearest allowed output rate. That is the hatched band, drawn to scale — a fraction of the daily cap.

One honest caveat: on current Gemini models reasoning depth is still a request parameter the client sets, and this implementation does not pin it. The bound holds while thought tokens stay inside the forced ceiling. Pin the thinking level too if you need it strict rather than indicative.

Presentation aid for the llm-gateway reference implementation. Every policy name, flow variable, fault code and default shown here is taken from proxy/llm-gateway/apiproxy/ and the example configuration in config/. Prices are the published Gemini list rates of 16 August 2026, standard service tier, global endpoint, USD per million tokens. Treat them as a snapshot, and note two things about them: the gemini-3.7-flash figures are a promotional 50% discount that runs until 1 January 2027, so that model's real cost doubles the day the promotion ends, with no change on your side to notice; and a non-global endpoint carries a 10% premium over the global rate, so the same model and the same token count are priced differently depending on where the request is sent. Re-check every rate against the pricing page before quoting a figure from this page.
